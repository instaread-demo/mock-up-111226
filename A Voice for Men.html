The Only Non-Gynocentric Approach in the Manosphere

Within the manosphere, every major school of thought—traditional MRAs, Red Pill and PUA communities, MGTOW, Black Pill and incel circles, and tradcon voices—treats gynocentrism as an evolutionary given. They differ in tactics, yet they share the premise that women (and to varying extents men) are hardwired for maintaining female-centered narcissism and solipsism, making extreme prioritization of women a permanent feature of human nature that can only be managed, navigated, withdrawn from, or fatalistically accepted.

Against this near-consensus stands Men’s Human Rights Advocacy (MHRA), or the Men’s Human Rights Movement (MHRM). Coined in 2013, MHRA is defined by the explicit rejection of that premise. It holds that while biological tendencies absolutely exist, the modern syndrome of gynocentrism is not a compulsory part of evolved female nature. It is a cultivated product of developmental failure and cultural indoctrination—historically amplified, especially from medieval courtly traditions onward—and therefore neither inevitable nor something women “cannot help.”

Only the MHRA label is founded on this non-gynocentric axiom. A small minority of individuals elsewhere in the manosphere may privately share the same view, but their groups as such do not.

Origins and definition of MHRA/MHRM

The terms Men’s Human Rights Advocacy (MHRA) and Men’s Human Rights Movement (MHRM) were introduced in January 2013 at A Voice for Men, with Paul Elam adopting the phrase in the article “Entering a New ERA,” while Peter Wright’s contemporaneous piece “Welcome to the Second Wave” framed the shift as a deliberate second wave of men’s advocacy.

Earlier (“first-wave”) men’s rights efforts frequently sought to defend men’s ability to fulfill traditional roles—provider, protector, or chivalric servant—while still operating inside longstanding cultural assumptions of male sacrifice and female prioritization. The 2013 coinage marked a clean break from that orientation, along with false biological theories used to underpin it. MHRA is defined by the explicit premise that men’s interests are human rights in their own right, not accommodations negotiated inside a system presumed to be biologically or evolutionarily woman-centered. It rejects the claim that humans must function within a bio-gynocentric order in which female centrality, male disposability, or chivalric service form the baseline reality.

Adding the word “human” was also intentional: it counters the common caricature of men’s advocacy as a bid for regressive rights over women and children, and it anchors the project in universal human-rights language rather than in gender-role traditionalism.

From the outset, the distinction was conceptual rather than merely tactical—MHRA is the only manosphere label coined and defined as non-gynocentric.

The hardwired-gynocentrism axiom across the manosphere

Across the rest of the manosphere, groups differ in tactics and tone, yet they share a common premise: gynocentrism—reducible to female-centered narcissism and solipsism as the governing forces of human relationships—is largely hardwired into female nature and is therefore a permanent feature of our species. Men, on this view, are compelled by evolutionary forces and reflexes either to play along with it or to wrestle with it—but they can never be without it. They disagree on how best to respond to it today, but they nevertheless accept it as evolutionary bedrock.

Traditional MRAs focus on concrete legal and institutional disadvantages—family courts, custody bias, false accusations, education gaps, suicide, male victims of domestic violence. Some refer to this as “Gamma bias,” and a substantial majority of these advocates nevertheless treat “natural gynocentrism” as given: women are evolutionarily more central, so the practical goal is to secure a less punitive deal for men inside a system that will remain woman-prioritizing. Reform is sought; the premise of female centrality is not abandoned.

Red Pill and PUA communities center on decoding female psychology, hypergamy, and dual mating strategies, then develop techniques of “game” to approach these supposedly timeless, static behaviors. Sexual market value, status, and methods for attracting or managing women are the metrics of success. The entire enterprise assumes female choice and gatekeeping are hardwired realities that men must adapt to, rather than contingent cultural patterns that could be otherwise. Like the armies of blue-pill men indoctrinated into the gynocentric script, those who play along with the PUA/game framework often invite confirmation bias by eliciting, through their own cues, the very behaviors they then cite as evidence of hardwired female nature.

MGTOW is grounded in the overriding principle of male self-determination, yet frequently set in explicit contradistinction and reaction to hardwired human gynocentrism. It advocates opting out of the expected social contract with women—refusing marriage, cohabitation, and often committed relationships—because of the legal, financial, and social risks created by a biologically driven gynocentric order. Many pursue more novel, lower-burden interactions that depart from the usual gynocentric script, yet the worldview that justifies these choices still commonly treats hypergamy, male sycophancy, and female prioritization as fixed evolutionary imperatives. Withdrawal or selective engagement remains rational either way—regardless of whether gynocentric traits are treated as permanent evolutionary facts or as a widespread cultural phenomenon of the current era. A mistaken premise can still produce a fitting response to the conditions actually in play.

Black Pill and incel communities, with few exceptions, tend push the same premise to its fatalistic extreme: female hypergamy and looks-based selection are genetically locked, male outcomes are largely predetermined, and little can be altered. Women remain the absolute arbiters of male value; the entire orientation is organized around that inescapable fact.

Tradcon and traditionalist strands within or adjacent to the manosphere often seek a return to complementary gender roles, male provision and protection, and chivalric norms. These are classic expressions of gynocentrism merely preferred in their older form over the feminist variant. Female elevation and male sacrifice are treated as God-given or evolutionarily proper—natural and desirable rather than culturally crafted and amplified.

In each case the organizing principle remains the same: gynocentrism is hardwired female nature, so strategy consists of navigating, reforming, withdrawing from, or resigning oneself to it as a permanent human condition.

Resistance still premised on permanence

Opposition to gynocentrism within the manosphere does not equal non-gynocentrism. The tactics differ—legal and institutional reform among traditional MRAs, selective withdrawal or novel lower-burden arrangements among MGTOW, technique-based navigation among Red Pill and PUA communities, fatalistic resignation among Black Pill and incel circles, or restoration of older chivalric forms among tradcons—yet each remains an attempt to manage, escape, or reconfigure a system still treated as biologically compulsory.

What unites these responses is the shared premise that gynocentrism is hardwired into female nature and therefore permanent. Resistance, on this view, means negotiating a better position inside it, minimizing exposure to it, or resigning oneself to its inevitability. It does not mean rejecting the claim that female-centered narcissism and solipsism are evolutionary givens. Even the most thorough opting-out remains rationalized by the belief that those traits cannot be escaped at the level of human nature itself.

Only by abandoning that premise does resistance become non-gynocentric. The MHRA position is defined by precisely that abandonment.

A common supporting move is the appeal to “praxeology”—the manosphere’s appropriated term for describing what is observably happening in the present moment. Current patterns of female-centered behavior are taken as sufficient evidence that gynocentrism may be the permanent human condition, while the fact that behavior changes from year to year, and even minute to minute, is set aside. The move closes off any serious consideration of non-gynocentric human interaction throughout the past, in the immediate present, or of possible non-gynocentric arrangements into the future, thereby protecting the confirmation bias that treats today’s praxeological samplings as proof of hardwired gynocentric nature.

Praxeology in this sense is only an observation of momentary, cross-cultural praxis. Process philosophers remind us that such a snapshot is a mirage: behavior is an ongoing movement of process that changes from second to second. Put differently, praxis is shaped and driven by the passing ideology and memetics of the moment. What is being described through cultural observations is largely a product of contemporary ideology, rather than a stable, unchanging essence around which a man can reliably orient his life. The assumption that such a snapshot reveals a static human nature denies that human behavior and culture are always in process—never fixed essences—capable of historical variation and future change.

The minority exceptions and the MHRA axiom

Within every major manosphere group a small minority of individuals—likely under five percent—already reject the claim that gynocentrism is hardwired female nature. They treat the modern syndrome of female-centered narcissism and solipsism as a product of developmental failure and cultural indoctrination rather than an evolutionary given. Some of these men, including a small strand inside the realms of MGTOW, therefore sit compatibly inside the same conceptual frame that defines MHRA.

Yet the existence of individual exceptions does not alter the character of the groups themselves. Traditional MRAs, Red Pill and PUA communities, MGTOW as a whole, Black Pill circles, and tradcon strands continue to operate on the shared premise that gynocentrism is permanent female nature. Their dominant discourses, strategies, and self-understandings remain organized around that premise.

Only the MHRA/MHRM label was coined and defined, from its 2013 origin onward, as explicitly premised on the opposite axiom: that gynocentrism is not women’s hardwired nature. Those who adopt the MHRA designation do so on that basis. The distinction is therefore not that no one elsewhere has reached the same conclusion, but that only MHRA is structured, named, and maintained as a perspective in which the rejection of bio-gynocentrism is foundational rather than incidental.

MGTOW as compatible demonstration of human-rights

MGTOW’s decision to opt out of the gynocentric social contract is an individualistic, human-rights gesture by men: an assertion of self-determination against other-determination. From the 2013 articulation of MHRA onward, this form of male self-determination has been treated as compatible with the intent of men’s human rights advocacy, not as a rival stance opposed to it.

Some men who identify as MHRA pursue cultural or institutional pushback against specific expressions of gynocentrism; others, just like MGTOW, treat personal non-participation as the primary response. Regardless of the response chosen, the continuous premise is a refusal to treat gynocentrism as an incontrovertible bio-social system that women cannot help enact, nor men support.

MGTOW self-determination therefore sits inside the same frame as an expression of a human-rights claim—I will live as I please. This is true in reference to the minority who reject the hardwired premise, but also as a practical option for those MGTOW who still hold to it. Withdrawal or selective, lower-burden engagement remains compatible with the MHRA axiom precisely because the axiom does not require men to remain inside a system organized around female centrality, nor does it insist on reforming the political system as a motive that was and remains standard for older MRAs—which is now considered only one way among many of tackling men’s human rights.

Gynocentrism as cultivated syndrome

Gynocentrism is a cultivated syndrome comprised of subclinical narcissism and solipsism, which are produced by cultural indoctrination and developmental failures. Isolated biological tendencies toward the protection and provisioning of family members—grandparents, women, children, men, relatives, or others—are misread as gynocentric priorities when it is women who are the occasional recipients, but these acts are more accurately classed as expressions of family love (Greek storge) in which care follows the shifting needs of family members rather than a fixed women-first hierarchy. What is rejected are the totalizing claims that humans are a gynocentric species, that male-female relationships are biologically designed to be woman-centered, or that the extreme modern form of gynocentrism is simply hardwired female nature.

The modern syndrome is treated instead as a culturally supersized trope. It draws on historical patterns—especially those that took shape in medieval courtly traditions and were later intensified through romantic and chivalric tropes, modern media, and social contracts—and on developmental exemptions that spare women from fully internalizing the reality principle. Solipsism, on this account, is more plausibly a failure of childhood psychological development peculiar to the modern era than an evolved inevitability that men must accommodate via constant self-improvement and giving.

The result is a gendered, subclinical expression of narcissistic traits within heterosexual and cultural contexts, not a compulsory species-level hardwiring. Recognition of this modern phenomenon, and of the practical wisdom of avoiding its excesses, does not require the explanatory story that “women cannot help it because it is their deep evolutionary nature.”

Conclusion

MHRA is the only perspective in the manosphere explicitly founded on the rejection of gynocentrism as hardwired female nature. Other groups may resist its effects through reform, withdrawal, technique, or fatalism, yet they do so while treating it as a permanent evolutionary given. Scattered individuals elsewhere may reach the same non-gynocentric conclusion, but only MHRA is coined and defined as wholly premised on that axiom. The distinction is conceptual: men’s human rights pursued without accepting female centrality as the inescapable baseline.

Peter Wright

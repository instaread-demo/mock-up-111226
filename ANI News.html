    Kevin Malloy replaces Diana Puketapu-Lyndon as New Zealand Cricket Chair

    ANI | Updated: Nov 12, 2026 15:58 IST

    Wellington [New Zealand], November 12 (ANI): Kevin Malloy has been elected Chair of New Zealand Cricket (NZC), succeeding Diana Puketapu-Lyndon, who stepped down from the Board at Thursday's 132nd Annual General Meeting having completed the maximum nine years as a director.Malloy, who has been an NZC Director since 2019 and served as Deputy Chair, was elected by the Board at a meeting held immediately following the AGM, according to a press release from NZC.He brings to the role a strong background in sport governance, alongside three decades of international commercial leadership, including 29 years with global media agency Starcom, based out of London, Hong Kong and New York, where he led the global Coca-Cola and Procter & Gamble accounts, and rose to become CEO of International Operations. Malloy is set to conclude a three-year term as Chair of Super Rugby Pacific in December and currently sits on the boards of Kiwibank and Tourism New Zealand.Puketapu-Lyndon joined the Board in 2017 and in 2023 became the first woman, and the first person of Māori descent, to chair NZC. On the recommendation of the Board Appointments Panel, Members elected Raewyn Lovett ONZM and Jono Brent to the Board, while Anna Campbell and Ali Shanks were re-elected after coming up for their three-year renewal.Mike Devonshire, whose term had also come up for renewal, was not recommended for reappointment by the Panel and leaves the Board after three years of service.Lovett brings more than two decades of governance experience in New Zealand and international sport, having chaired Sport New Zealand, High Performance Sport New Zealand and Netball New Zealand, as well as co-chairing the International Working Group on Women and Sport. Brent has had 20 years' experience as a Chartered Accountant as a member of CANZ from 2003-2023, and is a former chief executive in the infrastructure sector. He chaired Canterbury Cricket for three years and has spent the past 12 months leading the development of the Community Cricket New Zealand strategy.Malloy paid tribute to Puketapu-Lyndon and welcomed the new appointments.

    "I want to begin by thanking Diana, who has given nine years of service to this Board and led with real courage through one of the most demanding periods in the game's recent history,” he said."It is an honour to be asked to lead NZC as Chair and it’s a responsibility I take on with a strong understanding of how much the game means to so many New Zealanders. My thanks go to Mike Devonshire for his valuable contributions over the past three years, and I want to welcome Raewyn and Jono, who bring outstanding governance, financial, and community cricket experience to the table,” he added.Malloy said the Board was well aware that cricket, like many sports in New Zealand, must adapt to a changing world.“The landscape of cricket is evolving rapidly, both here and around the world, and while that brings challenges, it also brings opportunities. It’s an exciting time for NZC. We’re in the middle of a vibrant in-bound tour by India with record attendances, our BLACKCAPS and WHITE FERNS have major tours and ICC events on the horizon, and we are also preparing to co-host the ICC Men’s T20 World Cup in 2028,” said Malloy.Malloy acknowledged the level of interest in NZ20 and its potential, but said the Board was just as focused on the work going on across the community game and the high performance systems.“NZ20 is an aspirational concept with an incredible potential, which is why the Board chose it as the preferred option for the future of our domestic T20 competition. I know there’s plenty of work going on in that space and we're hopeful of some positive updates from the NZ20 Establishment Committee in the near future," he said.“More broadly, the Board understands that cricket has a job to do to grow our game. We know we are competing for attention against a myriad of other sports and entertainment products, so it’s upon us as an organisation to ensure Kiwis continue connecting with and loving cricket," he added.The 132nd NZC AGM also featured former NZC and ICC Chair Greg Barclay CNZM being elected a Life Member of NZC in recognition of his significant contribution to the game and sports governance, while former WHITE FERN Rachel Pullar, former BLACKCAP Gary Troup ONZM and international umpires Steve Dunne and Steve Woodward were appointed Honorary Members for their outstanding service to cricket in New Zealand.Former BLACKCAPS player and coach Mark Greatbatch continues in his role as NZC President for the second year of his three year term.Members also adopted NZC's audited financial statements for the year ended 31 July 2026, which confirmed a deficit of $7.3 million against a budgeted deficit of $1.8 million, as first advised in August. NZC's reserves stand at $25.2 million, and the organisation is forecasting a surplus of more than $10 million for FY27, driven largely by the India men's tour. (ANI)

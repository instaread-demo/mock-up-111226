Workplace Injury in the UAE: An Employer’s Guide to Reporting, Pay and Compensation

When an employee is hurt at work, the first hours matter for their recovery and for the employer’s legal position. In the UAE private sector, the response to a workplace injury follows clear rules on reporting, medical care, wages during treatment and compensation. Employers who understand those rules in advance can act quickly, support the injured worker and avoid disputes later.

What Counts as a Workplace Injury

Under Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021 on the Regulation of Labour Relations, a work injury covers physical harm suffered because of, or during, the performance of work, and occupational diseases linked to the job. Typical examples include machinery accidents, falls and vehicle incidents on duty, as well as illnesses caused by long-term exposure to dust or chemicals. Some incidents during work-related travel can also qualify, although commuting cases are less clear-cut and may need confirmation from the Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation (MOHRE). A warehouse worker who slips while loading stock and fractures a wrist is a straightforward case. A practical walkthrough of handling a workplace injury in the UAE offers further employer examples.

Immediate Steps and Reporting Deadlines

The first priority is the employee’s safety. Supervisors should give first aid, make the area safe, stop any machinery involved and call 998 for an ambulance if the injury is serious. The time, location and witnesses should be noted straight away. The employee should then be taken to a licensed medical facility, with a request for a report stating the cause and severity of the injury.

Reporting deadlines are strict. According to the UAE government portal, employers must report a work injury or occupational disease immediately to the police station covering the workplace’s location and inform MOHRE within 48 hours, which can be done through the ministry’s app and other official channels. The employer’s insurer should also be notified promptly, and every filing reference number kept on record. Free zone employers should check whether their authority has an additional reporting step.

Medical Costs and Wages During Treatment

The employer is responsible for the cost of treating the injury until the employee recovers or a permanent disability is assessed. Mandatory health insurance may overlap with this duty, so it is worth clarifying with the insurer which costs fall under each.

If the injury prevents the employee from working, the law protects their income. The employer must pay the full wage for the treatment period or six months, whichever is shorter. If treatment continues beyond six months, the employee receives half wage for up to a further six months, or until recovery, a confirmed disability or death, whichever comes first. An employee off work for eight weeks after a fracture would therefore remain on full pay throughout.

Compensation for Disability or Death

Where a work injury or occupational disease causes death, the worker’s family is entitled to compensation equal to 24 months of basic wage, subject to a minimum of AED 18,000 and a maximum of AED 200,000. Permanent total disability attracts the same level of compensation. For partial disability, the amount is a percentage of that figure, based on the official disability schedules set by Cabinet Resolution No. 33 of 2022. The law also lists circumstances in which compensation is not payable, such as deliberate self-injury or injury caused by intoxication, so each case should be assessed on its facts.

Disputes over disability percentages or payments should start with the company’s internal process. Unresolved cases can go to MOHRE, which since amendments in 2024 can issue binding decisions on labour claims up to AED 50,000, with larger claims referred to the courts.

Records and Risks

Good records support every later step. Each incident file should include the medical report, incident form, photographs, witness statements, payment receipts, and leave and salary records. A root cause review, followed by a fix such as anti-slip flooring rather than another warning sign, reduces the chance of a repeat. Late or missing reports can lead to penalties, insurance disputes and a weaker position in any claim.

Conclusion

Handling a workplace injury in the UAE requires fast care, timely reporting to the police and MOHRE, correct wage payments and accurate records. A short written procedure, a named responsible person and valid insurance make that response far easier. Companies operating from free zones such as Meydan Free Zone should build these steps into their health and safety policy before their first employee starts.
